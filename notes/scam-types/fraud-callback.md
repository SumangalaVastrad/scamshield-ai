# Fraud alert callback and IVR scam

## Short description
Scammers send alarming messages or automated robocalls claiming an unauthorized purchase (e.g. $450 or Rs 48,000) was detected on your "card ending in 1234". The message directs you to "press 1" or dial an unverified phone number to speak to the bank fraud prevention department.

## How it usually looks
"Bank Fraud Alert: Did you authorize charge of $499.00 on card ending 4921 at Amazon? If NO, press 1 immediately to speak with fraud prevention or call 1800-XXX-XXXX. Please stay on the line."

## Red flags
- Asks you to dial a phone number or press an IVR digit provided inside the message rather than calling the official number on the back of your debit card.
- Fake executive creates extreme urgency and insists you "stay on the line" while they process a cancellation.
- Executive asks for your One-Time Password (OTP), CVV, or card PIN to "refund" or "reverse" the charge.
- Banks never need your OTP, PIN, or CVV to block or cancel a transaction.

## What to do now (not yet paid or shared anything)
1. Hang up the call immediately. Do not press any IVR digits (do not press 1).
2. Check your actual bank mobile app or NetBanking statement to see if any charge actually occurred.
3. Call your bank using ONLY the verified emergency customer care number printed on the back of your physical card.
4. Block and report the fraudulent phone number.

## What not to do
- Never share OTPs, CVVs, or card PINs with anyone claiming to be from bank fraud detection.
- Never install AnyDesk, QuickSupport, or screen-sharing tools to reverse a charge.

## What to do if already clicked, shared, or paid (Urgent)
1. Immediately call your bank using the number on your card to freeze accounts, hotlist cards, and block online banking.
2. Dial 1930 or submit an incident report at cybercrime.gov.in.
3. Preserve call records and message screenshots as evidence.
