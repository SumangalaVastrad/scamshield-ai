# Fake customer care number

## Short description
Fraudsters manipulate Google search engine results and Google Maps listings to display their personal phone numbers as official customer support for banks, airlines, couriers, food delivery apps, or e-commerce platforms. When victims call for help, scammers steal their money or credentials.

## How it usually looks
Victim searches on Google for "Swiggy refund customer care" or "SBI helpline", calls the first mobile number listed, and the person answers posing as an executive: "To process your refund of Rs 450, please download our verification app or click this UPI link."

## Red flags
- Official customer support numbers for large institutions are rarely 10-digit standard personal mobile numbers.
- Agent instructs you to download AnyDesk or click a link to process a simple grievance.
- Agent asks you to enter your UPI PIN or pay a token registration fee.

## What to do now (not yet paid or shared anything)
1. Always obtain customer support contacts exclusively from the verified official app or official website URL typed manually.
2. Hang up if an agent asks you to scan a QR code, pay a fee, or install software.
3. Report the fraudulent listing on Google Maps or search results.

## What not to do
- Never trust phone numbers displayed in Google search ads, image results, or unverified social media profiles.
- Never enter your card details or PIN to receive a refund.

## What to do if already clicked, shared, or paid (Urgent)
1. Call your actual bank's official number (from the back of your card) immediately to block your accounts and cards.
2. Disconnect remote access and change all credentials.
3. Lodge an immediate complaint on the national cybercrime portal with the phone number and transaction details.
