# Impersonation and digital arrest

## Short description
Fraudsters impersonate law enforcement, CBI, police, customs, or telecom authorities (TRAI) on phone or video calls. They falsely accuse the victim of sending illegal parcels containing drugs/passports or running money laundering accounts, place them under fake "digital arrest", and coerce them into transferring funds for "verification".

## How it usually looks
"This is Inspector Sharma from Mumbai Cyber Crime Branch. A FedEx parcel sent in your name containing 5 passports and illegal drugs was seized by Customs. A non-bailable arrest warrant has been issued against you. You are under digital arrest. Do not disconnect this video call."

## Red flags
- **There is NO provision for 'digital arrest' under Indian law.** No legitimate police officer or judicial authority will arrest or interrogate anyone over WhatsApp or Skype video calls.
- Demands that you transfer your savings to a "secure RBI account" or "court verification escrow".
- High psychological coercion, isolation from family, and threats of immediate physical arrest.

## What to do now (not yet paid or shared anything)
1. **Remain calm and hang up the call immediately.**
2. Real government agencies send formal written notices and summons; they never demand money over video calls.
3. Call 1930 (Cybercrime Helpline) or visit your nearest local police station to report the intimidation.
4. Block the caller's number.

## What not to do
- Never transfer any money for "bail", "case clearance", or "RBI verification".
- Do not stay isolated; immediately talk to your family or friends.

## What to do if already clicked, shared, or paid (Urgent)
1. Immediately contact the national cybercrime helpline (1930) and report the transaction details to freeze beneficiary bank accounts.
2. Alert your bank branch manager to trace the recipient account.
3. File a formal complaint at the nearest cyber police station with call recordings and account details.
